**The short answer:** for most Wilmington, NC businesses, **TPO** is the best all-around flat roofing system — reflective, heat-welded, 20–30 year service life, and moderate cost. Choose **PVC** for restaurants and chemical exposure, **EPDM** when you want proven flexibility and simple repairs, **modified bitumen** for small or complex roofs with heavy foot traffic, and **metal** when long service life and large spans justify the premium.

The membrane is only half the decision. Attachment, insulation, drainage, flashing and edge metal determine whether a “good” membrane survives a hurricane season.

## What is the best flat roofing system for a Wilmington business?

TPO is usually the best flat roofing system for a Wilmington business: reflective, heat-welded, 20 to 30 years of service life, and moderate cost. Choose PVC for restaurants, EPDM for proven flexibility, modified bitumen for heavy foot traffic, or metal for long service life. The full comparison is below.

## The five systems, compared

| System | Best for | Service life | Relative cost | Watch-outs |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| TPO | Retail, office, warehouse, multifamily | 20 – 30 years | Moderate | Seam quality depends on installer skill; grease exposure shortens life |
| PVC | Restaurants, kitchens, chemical exposure | 20 – 30 years | Higher than TPO | Higher material cost; fewer local installers |
| EPDM | Offices, warehouses, simple repairs | 20 – 30 years | Moderate | Black surface increases cooling load; adhesive seams need care |
| Modified bitumen | Small or complex roofs, foot traffic | 15 – 25 years | Moderate to high | More labor-intensive; heavier assembly |
| Metal (low-slope) | Industrial, large spans, long ownership | 30 – 50+ years | High | Corrosion detailing matters near the coast |

### TPO: the practical default

Thermoplastic polyolefin is a single-ply membrane with heat-welded seams and a reflective surface. For a typical Wilmington retail center, office building, or multifamily property, it offers the best balance of upfront cost, energy performance, and service life.

Specify the details that matter: membrane thickness, attachment pattern for wind uplift, cover board, insulation R-value, and heat-welded flashing. A thin TPO roof installed with generic perimeter details will not perform like an engineered coastal assembly.

### PVC: the restaurant and kitchen choice

PVC costs more than TPO, but it resists grease, oils, and many chemicals. If the building has a commercial kitchen, rooftop exhaust, or food processing, the premium is usually justified. Ask for heat-welded seams and chemical-resistant walkway details around equipment.

### EPDM: proven and repairable

EPDM is a flexible rubber membrane with a long track record. It performs well in temperature swings and is straightforward to repair. The trade-off is a dark surface that increases cooling load and adhesive or tape seams that depend on careful installation. It remains a sound choice for warehouses and offices where energy reflection is not the priority.

### Modified bitumen: redundancy for complex roofs

Modified bitumen uses multiple reinforced layers, which provides useful redundancy on smaller or heavily accessed roofs. It is more labor-intensive than single-ply, but it handles complex geometry and frequent foot traffic well.

### Metal: long-term value on the right building

Standing-seam metal is a strong option for industrial buildings and large spans where a 30–50+ year service life justifies the cost. Near the ocean, specify a corrosion-resistant coating and fasteners, and pay attention to condensation control and fastener isolation.

## A simple decision framework

| Building situation | Recommended starting point |
|---|---|
| Retail, office, or multifamily, budget-conscious | TPO with enhanced attachment |
| Restaurant, commercial kitchen, food service | PVC |
| Warehouse or office, simple geometry, repair-friendly | EPDM |
| Small, complex roof with equipment and foot traffic | Modified bitumen |
| Industrial, large span, long ownership horizon | Standing-seam metal |

If the building is occupied, add phasing and protection to the decision. A system that requires a full tear-off may cost more in business disruption than a recover — but only if a moisture survey confirms the existing roof is dry enough to recover.

## What matters more than the membrane brand

1. **Wind uplift.** Attachment and edge metal must match the building's exposure. This is engineered, not guessed.
2. **Drainage.** Ponding water is the most common cause of premature failure. Fix drains and scuppers during the project.
3. **Insulation.** R-value affects energy cost and condensation. Wet insulation must be removed.
4. **Flashing and penetrations.** Curbs, pipe boots, HVAC supports and wall terminations are where leaks begin.
5. **Warranty.** Compare manufacturer and workmanship coverage, exclusions, and who handles claims.

A premium membrane installed over poor drainage or attached incorrectly will fail sooner than a well-installed, less expensive system.

## Wilmington-specific considerations

- **Hurricane season** runs June through November. A roof installed in spring has a full season to prove its seams and flashing.
- **Salt air** accelerates corrosion on fasteners, edge metal, and metal panels within a few miles of the ocean.
- **Heavy rain** exposes undersized drains and scuppers. A roof that ponds after a summer storm is telling you something.
- **Humidity** makes attic and roof-deck ventilation important, even on low-slope assemblies.
- **Insurance** may reward FORTIFIED or wind-mitigation details. Ask your carrier before you specify.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is TPO or EPDM better for a Wilmington NC commercial roof?

TPO is usually the better default for Wilmington businesses because its reflective surface reduces cooling load and its heat-welded seams perform well in high heat and humidity. EPDM is a strong alternative when the building needs proven flexibility and simple repairs. The right choice depends on the building use, existing assembly, and attachment requirements.

### What flat roof system is best for a restaurant in Wilmington?

PVC is usually the best choice for restaurants and commercial kitchens because it resists grease, oils, and many chemicals that shorten the life of TPO or EPDM. Kitchen exhaust and rooftop cooking equipment make chemical resistance the deciding factor, even though PVC costs more upfront.

### How long does a commercial flat roof last in coastal North Carolina?

A properly installed TPO, PVC, or EPDM roof typically lasts 20 to 30 years in coastal North Carolina. Metal low-slope roofs can last 30 to 50 years or more. Actual life depends on attachment, drainage, insulation, foot traffic, maintenance, and whether the assembly was specified for wind and salt exposure.
