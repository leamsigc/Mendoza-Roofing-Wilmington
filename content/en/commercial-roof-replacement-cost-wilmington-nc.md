**The short answer:** commercial roof replacement in Wilmington, NC costs roughly **$5 to $15 per square foot** installed, or about **$7 to $13 per square foot all-in** for a typical coastal project. A 5,000 sq ft roof budgets at **$35,000–$65,000**; 10,000 sq ft at **$70,000–$130,000**; 20,000 sq ft at **$140,000–$260,000**; and 50,000 sq ft at **$350,000–$650,000**.

Those ranges are for budgeting, not bidding. The spread between a $60,000 and a $95,000 quote on the same building is usually scope, not profit: insulation, deck repairs, drainage, edge metal, and how much tear-off is included.

## How much does commercial roof replacement cost in Wilmington, NC?

Commercial roof replacement in Wilmington, NC typically costs $5 to $15 per square foot installed, or about $7 to $13 per square foot all-in. A 5,000 sq ft roof runs $35,000 to $65,000; 10,000 sq ft $70,000 to $130,000. System, insulation, drainage and tear-off drive the number.

## Cost per square foot by roof system

| System | Installed cost | Typical use |
|---|---|---|
| TPO single-ply | $5 – $9 per sq ft | Retail, office, warehouse, multifamily |
| EPDM rubber | $5 – $9 per sq ft | Offices, warehouses, proven low-slope performance |
| PVC membrane | $6 – $11 per sq ft | Restaurants, kitchens, chemical exposure |
| Modified bitumen | $5 – $10 per sq ft | Smaller or complex roofs, heavy foot traffic |
| Metal (low-slope) | $9 – $18+ per sq ft | Industrial, large spans, long service life |
| Replacement with extensive repairs | $10 – $20+ per sq ft | Deteriorated deck, wet insulation, drainage upgrades |

These figures include membrane, insulation, attachment, flashing, and normal cleanup. They do not include structural repairs, asbestos abatement, or major electrical and HVAC relocation.

| Roof area | Budget range | Typical building |

A 50,000 sq ft roof is not simply 10 times a 5,000 sq ft roof. Large projects benefit from crew efficiency but often carry more complex phasing, more penetrations, and stricter safety requirements.

## What moves the price in Wilmington

**Wind uplift.** New Hanover and Brunswick County roofs must resist hurricane-season wind. Attachment patterns, edge metal, and perimeter details are engineered, not generic. A high-exposure building near the water can require a more robust — and more expensive — assembly.

**Salt air and corrosion.** Fasteners, edge metal, and flashing near the coast need corrosion-resistant materials. Saving a few thousand dollars on standard fasteners is a poor trade on a beachfront property.

**Moisture and insulation.** Wet insulation has to be removed, not covered. A moisture survey before the bid prevents a cheap recover from trapping water against the deck.

**Drainage.** Ponding water, undersized drains, and failed scuppers are common on older Wilmington commercial roofs. Tapered insulation and new drainage can add 10–20% to a project.

**Tear-off versus recover.** A recover avoids disposal costs and can be faster, but it is only appropriate when the existing assembly is dry, the deck is sound, and code allows another layer. Many coastal roofs fail that test.

**Access and occupancy.** Crane picks, limited staging, tenant protection, night work, and phasing all add labor. A vacant warehouse is cheaper to roof than an occupied restaurant.

## What a complete commercial proposal should include

- Roof plan with dimensions and penetration count.
- Existing assembly and tear-off or recover scope.
- Insulation type, thickness, and R-value.
- Membrane manufacturer, thickness, and attachment method.
- Flashing, edge metal, drains, scuppers, and curbs.
- Deck repair unit rates and wet insulation allowances.
- Phasing, schedule, and protection for tenants and equipment.
- Manufacturer and workmanship warranty terms.
- Payment milestones, lien waivers, and change-order process.

If a proposal is missing more than two of these, it is not ready to compare.

## Three ways to control the budget

1. **Fix the cause before you replace.** A roof that ponds because a drain is clogged or a scupper is undersized will pond again on a new membrane. Correct drainage during the project.
2. **Phase the work.** If the building is occupied, phasing can spread cost across budget years and keep tenants operating.
3. **Choose the system for the building, not the brochure.** TPO is a sound default for many Wilmington buildings; PVC earns its premium in restaurants; metal makes sense when service life and span justify it.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the average cost per square foot for a commercial roof in Wilmington NC?

Most Wilmington commercial roof replacements fall between $5 and $15 per square foot installed. TPO and EPDM usually land at $5 to $9, PVC at $6 to $11, modified bitumen at $5 to $10, and metal at $9 to $18 or more. Projects with extensive deck or insulation repairs can exceed $20 per square foot.

### How much does a 10,000 square foot commercial roof cost?

A 10,000 square foot commercial roof in coastal North Carolina typically budgets at $70,000 to $130,000, based on a planning range of $7 to $13 per square foot. The final number depends on the membrane, insulation, attachment, tear-off, drainage work, and condition of the deck.

### Is a roof recover cheaper than a full tear-off?

A recover is often less expensive because it avoids tear-off and disposal, but it is only allowed when the existing roof, deck, insulation, and code requirements permit it. A moisture survey is required first. If the insulation is wet or the building has too many existing layers, a full tear-off is the safer long-term choice.
