## What Wilmington commercial buildings actually need

Coastal New Hanover and Brunswick County roofs face conditions that inland bids often miss:

- **Wind uplift.** Attachment patterns, edge metal, and perimeter details must be engineered for the building's exposure.
- **Salt air.** Fasteners, edge metal, and flashing within a few miles of the ocean need corrosion-resistant materials.
- **Heavy rain and humidity.** Drains, scuppers, insulation, and condensation control matter as much as the membrane.
- **Occupied buildings.** Retail, restaurants, clinics, and offices need phasing, tenant protection, and daily watertight conditions.
- **Older roof assemblies.** Many Wilmington commercial roofs have multiple layers, wet insulation, or deck deterioration that must be found before a recover is specified.

Ask every contractor how they handle those five issues on a building like yours. The answer tells you more than the company brochure.

## How to compare commercial roofing companies

### 1. Commercial experience

Ask for three comparable projects completed in the last three years: same building type, similar size, same roof system. Then call the references. A residential roofer who has done one strip mall is not a commercial specialist.

### 2. Scope and technical quality

Compare membrane, insulation R-value, cover board, attachment, flashing, drainage, edge metal, deck repairs, and disposal. If one bid is silent on wet insulation or drainage, it is not cheaper — it is incomplete.

### 3. Licensing, insurance, and safety

Request current general liability and workers' compensation certificates. Confirm the limits with the insurer, not just the contractor. For occupied buildings, ask about subcontractor coverage, fall protection, and hot-work procedures.

### 4. Warranty and long-term support

Compare manufacturer and workmanship warranties, exclusions, maintenance requirements, transferability, and who handles claims. A long warranty with exclusions for ponding, foot traffic, or improper maintenance is not the protection it appears to be.

### 5. Project management and disruption

Ask for a written phasing plan, the maximum area opened per day, the watertight procedure, a named supervisor, and daily communication. On an occupied property, this is where projects succeed or fail.

### 6. Financial transparency

Require an itemized proposal with allowances, exclusions, payment milestones, change-order rates, and lien waivers. Avoid large upfront payments and vague “additional work” language.

## Questions to ask each company

1. How many commercial roofs like mine have you completed in the last three years?
2. Can you provide three commercial references I can call?
3. Who is the manufacturer certified installer for the proposed system?
4. What is excluded from this price, and what are your unit rates for deck and insulation repairs?
5. What is the maximum roof area you will open in one day?
6. Who supervises the job, and who do I call after hours?
7. What are the manufacturer and workmanship warranty terms?
8. How do you handle a leak during construction?
9. What is the payment schedule, and will you provide lien waivers?
10. What maintenance does the warranty require?
