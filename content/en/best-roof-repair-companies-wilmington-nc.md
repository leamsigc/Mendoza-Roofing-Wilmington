## What separates a good repair company from a cheap one

| Criterion | Why it matters | What to look for |
|---|---|---|
| Leak diagnosis | Guessing leads to repeat visits | Moisture readings, attic inspection, photos, a written cause |
| Coastal experience | Wind-driven rain and salt air create different failures | Local references, flashing and corrosion details, storm work |
| Written scope | Protects you from vague change orders | Materials, quantities, decking rates, cleanup, warranty |
| Workmanship warranty | Shows confidence in the repair | Repeat-leak coverage in writing, clear exclusions |
| Insurance and licensing | Protects you if someone is hurt or the work fails | Current COI, workers' comp, NC licensing where required |
| Responsiveness | Leaks do not wait | Local crew, answered phone, emergency process |
| Cleanup and respect | You live in the house | Protection, magnet sweeps, daily cleanup |

A company that is strong on price but weak on diagnosis and warranty is not a bargain. The second repair costs more than the first.

## How to compare three estimates

Ask each company to price the **same written scope**. Then compare:

1. **Diagnosis** — did they identify the same source?
2. **Scope** — materials, flashing, underlayment, decking, and cleanup.
3. **Price** — fixed vs. time-and-materials, and unit rates for hidden damage.
4. **Warranty** — length, coverage, exclusions, transferability.
5. **Schedule** — when they can start and how long it will take.
6. **References** — at least two recent local repairs.

If one estimate is dramatically lower, find out what is missing before you celebrate.

## Questions to ask on the phone

- Do you handle repairs, or only full replacements?
- Can you trace an active leak, or do you only replace shingles?
- Are you licensed and insured in North Carolina?
- Will you provide a written scope and photos?
- What warranty covers this repair?
- Can you provide two local references from similar work?
- What is your emergency response time?
- Do you use employees or subcontractors?

## Red flags

- A quote before anyone inspects the roof or attic.
- A recommendation to replace the entire roof without explaining why a repair is not viable.
- No written estimate, no photos, no warranty.
- A demand for full payment upfront.
- A crew that cannot provide a local address or references.
- Pressure to file an insurance claim before damage is documented.
- A price that changes after the work starts without a written change order.
