**The short answer:** compare roof repair warranties on what is covered, not on the number of years. Look at workmanship versus manufacturer coverage, labor and materials, repeat leaks, storm exclusions, interior water damage, transferability, and the written claim process. A two-year warranty that covers repeat leaks can be worth more than a ten-year warranty full of exclusions.

Wilmington's coastal weather makes the fine print matter. A warranty that excludes wind-driven rain, hurricane damage, or “improper maintenance” can leave you paying for the same leak twice.

## Workmanship versus manufacturer coverage

There are two warranties on almost every roof repair, and they cover different things:

| Warranty | Who backs it | What it covers | What it does not cover |
|---|---|---|---|
| Workmanship | The roofing contractor | Leaks or defects caused by the repair or installation labor | Material defects, storm damage, lack of maintenance, unrelated areas |
| Manufacturer | The shingle, membrane, or flashing maker | Defects in the product itself | Improper installation, poor ventilation, storm damage, labor in most cases |

Ask for both documents. A contractor who says “the shingles are warrantied for 30 years” is describing a product warranty, not a promise that your repair will stay leak-free for 30 years.

## The nine terms to compare

| Term | What to check |
|---|---|
| Workmanship length | One, two, five or ten years — and when does the clock start? |
| Labor and materials | Does the roofer pay for diagnosis, labor and replacement materials? |
| Repeat leaks | Will they return for the same leak at no charge? |
| Storm exclusions | Are wind, hurricanes, wind-driven rain and debris excluded? |
| Interior damage | Are ceilings, insulation and drywall covered, or only roof work? |
| Transferability | Can the warranty pass to a buyer, and is there a fee? |
| Maintenance requirements | Does the warranty require annual inspections or specific maintenance? |
| Claim process | How do you report a problem, and what is the response time? |
| Company backing | Contractor, manufacturer, or both — and who pays if the company closes? |

## Five questions to ask before signing

1. **Will you warrant this exact repair?** Ask them to name the flashing, boots, valleys, shingles, and decking they will touch.
2. **If the same leak returns, will you fix it free?** Clarify diagnostic labor, materials, and how many return visits are covered.
3. **What happens after a hurricane or tropical storm?** Distinguish storm damage from a workmanship failure that shows up during heavy rain.
4. **Does the warranty cover interior damage?** Many agreements cover roof work only, not the ceiling or insulation below it.
5. **What happens if the company closes?** Ask whether any manufacturer or third-party protection remains.

## Coastal exclusions to read twice

- **Wind-driven rain.** Some warranties exclude leaks caused by rain driven sideways under shingles or flashing. In a coastal market, that is a meaningful exclusion.
- **Hurricane and named-storm damage.** Usually excluded and covered by insurance instead. That is normal, but the definition of “storm damage” should be clear.
- **Ponding water.** Flat and low-slope repairs may exclude leaks caused by standing water or clogged drains.
- **Foot traffic and equipment.** Damage from HVAC service, solar installers, or regular rooftop access may be excluded.
- **Lack of maintenance.** A warranty can require gutter cleaning, drain clearing, or annual inspection. Missing one may void coverage.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between a workmanship warranty and a manufacturer warranty?

A workmanship warranty is the roofer's promise to fix leaks or defects caused by installation or repair labor. A manufacturer warranty covers defects in the roofing material itself. A shingle manufacturer will not pay to replace a properly made shingle that was installed incorrectly, and a roofer's warranty will not cover a product defect. Ask for both documents in writing.

### Does a roof repair warranty cover storm damage?

Usually not. Most workmanship warranties exclude damage caused by hurricanes, wind, hail, fallen debris and other storms, because that damage is insurable under a homeowner or commercial property policy. Read the exclusions and ask how the contractor distinguishes a workmanship failure from storm damage.

### Should a roof repair warranty be transferable?

Transferability matters if you may sell the property. Ask whether the warranty can transfer to a new owner, whether a fee or notification is required, and how many years of coverage remain. A transferable workmanship warranty can be a genuine selling point; a non-transferable one ends with you.
