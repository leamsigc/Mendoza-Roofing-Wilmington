## Wind-rated installation: what to ask

A “hurricane-rated” shingle is only part of the system. Ask how the contractor handles:

- **Deck attachment** — nail pattern and sheathing fastening.
- **Underlayment** — synthetic or peel-and-stick, and where it is required.
- **Starter course and ridge caps** — wind resistance at the edges.
- **Flashing** — valleys, walls, chimneys, skylights, and pipe boots.
- **Ventilation** — intake and exhaust balance to reduce heat and moisture.
- **Sealed roof deck** — a FORTIFIED detail that helps prevent water intrusion if shingles lift.

The installation specification should be in the written proposal, not just the sales conversation.

## FORTIFIED Roof: is it worth it?

FORTIFIED is a set of construction standards from the Insurance Institute for Business & Home Safety (IBHS). For coastal homes, the main benefits are a sealed roof deck, enhanced fastening, and wind-rated details that reduce water intrusion during a hurricane.

Ask for the FORTIFIED upgrade priced separately from a standard replacement, then ask your insurer whether it qualifies for a premium discount or wind-mitigation credit. Do not assume a discount; confirm it in writing with your carrier.

## Red flags

- A one-page quote with no material specification.
- No written deck-repair rate.
- A workmanship warranty that is only verbal.
- A request for full payment before the roof is installed.
- No local address, license, or insurance certificate.
- A crew that cannot name the manufacturer or product.
- Pressure to sign “today” for a discount.
- A proposal that does not mention ventilation or flashing.
