## Commercial Roofing Questions

### How can a business compare commercial roofing contractors?

Compare commercial roofing contractors on six weighted criteria: commercial experience, scope and technical quality, licensing, insurance and safety, warranty support, project management, and financial transparency. Give every bidder the same written scope, then compare total lifecycle cost rather than the lowest bid. Request three comparable projects and current certificates of insurance.

**What to score, and how much it should count:**

| Criterion | Weight | What to verify |
|---|---|---|
| Commercial experience | 25% | Three comparable projects in the last three years; references from similar building types and roof systems |
| Scope and technical quality | 20% | Tear-off vs. recover, insulation, membrane, flashing, drainage, edge metal, decking and wet insulation |
| Licensing, insurance, safety | 20% | NC licensing where required, general liability, workers' compensation, fall protection and hot-work procedures |
| Warranty and long-term support | 15% | Manufacturer and workmanship terms, exclusions, transferability, who handles claims |
| Project management and disruption | 10% | Schedule, phasing, weather protection, tenant coordination, daily cleanup, named supervisor |
| Financial transparency | 10% | Itemized proposal, allowances, exclusions, payment milestones, change-order rates |

**Normalize the scope before comparing price.** One bid can look cheaper because it excludes wet insulation replacement, drainage upgrades, or critical flashing work. Ask every bidder to explain exclusions and additional-work rates in writing.

**Red flags:** a one-line estimate, no verifiable commercial references, no current insurance certificate, a manufacturer warranty that does not match the proposed system, large upfront payments, or pressure to sign before you have compared bids.

The National Roofing Contractors Association (NRCA) recommends prequalifying contractors, checking insurance, obtaining references, and reviewing written proposals and warranties. See the [NRCA contractor-selection guidance](https://www.nrca.net/roofing-guidelines/selecting-a-contractor).

## Roof Repair Questions

### How can I compare roof repair warranties in Wilmington, NC?

Compare roof repair warranties on what is covered, not just the number of years: workmanship versus manufacturer coverage, labor and materials, recurring leaks, storm exclusions, interior water damage, transferability and the written claim process. A two-year warranty that covers repeat leaks can be worth more than a ten-year warranty full of exclusions.

| Warranty detail | What to check |
|---|---|
| Workmanship coverage | Does it cover leaks caused by faulty repair or installation? |
| Length and start date | One, two, five or ten years — and when does the clock start? |
| Labor and materials | Does the roofer pay for diagnosis, labor and replacement materials? |
| Recurring leaks | Will they return for the same leak at no charge? |
| Storm exclusions | Are wind, hurricane, wind-driven rain and debris excluded? |
| Interior damage | Are ceilings, insulation and drywall covered, or only roof work? |
| Transferability | Does coverage pass to a new owner if the property sells? |
| Company backing | Contractor, manufacturer, or both — and who pays claims? |
| Claim process | How do you report a problem, and what is the response time? |

**Five questions to ask before signing:**

1. Will you warrant this exact repair, including the flashing, boots, valleys and shingles you touch?
2. If the same leak returns, will you fix it free — including diagnostic labor?
3. What happens after a hurricane or tropical storm — is that excluded?
4. Does the warranty cover interior water damage?
5. What happens if the company closes — is any manufacturer or third-party protection left?

A manufacturer's shingle warranty covers product defects, not the roofer's workmanship. Ask for both documents.

### What should I ask a roofing contractor before hiring them for repairs?

Ask a roofing contractor for the cause of the problem and photo evidence, a written scope and itemized estimate, NC licensing and current liability and workers compensation certificates, materials and installation details, the workmanship warranty in writing, who performs the work, the schedule and cleanup plan, payment terms, and how storm damage will be documented for insurance.

**The 10-question checklist:**

| Question | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| What is causing the problem? | A repair that does not address the source will fail again |
| Can you show me photos? | Evidence of the damage and the proposed repair area |
| Is this a repair or a symptom of a bigger issue? | Hidden decking, attic moisture or widespread failure changes the scope |
| Are you licensed and insured? | Ask for current liability and workers' compensation certificates |
| Who performs the work — employees or subcontractors? | Accountability and warranty coverage |
| What exactly is included in the price? | Labor, materials, flashing, decking, disposal, cleanup, permits |
| What happens if you find hidden damage? | Written change-order rates and approval before extra work |
| What is the workmanship warranty? | Get terms in writing, including repeat-leak coverage |
| When will you start and how long will it take? | Weather plan, temporary protection, daily cleanup |
| How will you document storm damage? | Dated photos and a written scope your insurer can use |

**Red flags:** no written estimate, full payment upfront, refusal to show insurance, a recommendation to replace the whole roof without explaining why repair is not viable, scare tactics, or promises that insurance will definitely pay.

3. If water is near electrical fixtures or a sagging ceiling, stay out of the room and shut off power only if it is safe to do so.
4. Photograph the damage from the ground and inside the house.
5. Call your insurer to open a claim after you have stopped the active leak.

**What to ask the dispatcher:**

- Can you send someone today or right now?
- Can you stop the intrusion with emergency tarping or a temporary repair?
- Is there an after-hours callout fee or minimum charge?
- Can you provide photos and a written report for my insurance claim?

### When should a Wilmington homeowner replace rather than repair a roof?

Replace rather than repair when the roof is 20 or more years old, leaks recur in multiple areas, shingles are widely failing, decking is soft or sagging, or repairs approach 30 to 40 percent of replacement cost. Repair when the roof is under 15 years old, damage is isolated and the deck is sound. A free inspection should compare both options.

| Situation | Better starting point |
|---|---|
| Roof under 10–15 years old, one isolated leak | Repair |
| A few shingles lifted or missing after wind | Repair and inspect nearby areas |
| One failed flashing, boot or valley | Repair the source |
| Roof 20+ years old with widespread granule loss, curling or cracking | Replacement |
| Leaks in multiple rooms or recurring in the same area | Replacement |
| Soft, sagging or rotted decking | Replacement, including deck repairs |
| Repairs approaching 30–40% of replacement cost | Compare replacement |

**Wilmington-specific factors:** wind and tropical storms lift edges and fasteners; humidity and persistent leaks rot decking and insulation; salt air and sun age materials faster near the coast. After a significant storm, ask for an inspection of roof edges, flashing, fasteners and lifted shingles.

**Make the financial comparison:** if a $1,200 repair buys years on a sound roof, repair. If the roof is old, failing in several places, and likely to need another major repair soon, replacement is usually the better long-term value. Ask for photos, a repair-vs-replacement comparison, remaining roof life, and warranty terms in writing.

### Which roofing materials are best for homes near Wilmington, NC?

For homes near Wilmington, NC, architectural asphalt shingles offer the best value, standing-seam metal offers the best coastal durability with a 40 to 70 year lifespan, and clay or concrete tile offers the longest service life. Specify high wind ratings, corrosion-resistant fasteners, sealed roof decking and FORTIFIED details; salt exposure and wind uplift matter as much as the material.

| Material | Upfront cost | Lifespan | Coastal performance | Best use |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Architectural asphalt shingles | Low–moderate | 25 – 30 years | Good with high-wind installation and corrosion-resistant nails | Most homes and budgets |
| Impact-resistant shingles | Moderate | 25 – 30 years | Better hail and wind resistance | Storm-prone areas, insurance considerations |
| Standing-seam metal | High | 40 – 70 years | Excellent — concealed fasteners, wind-rated panels | Coastal and exposed properties |
| Corrugated metal | Moderate–high | 40 – 50 years | Good; specify coastal-grade coating and fasteners | Long-term value, rural and inland homes |
| Clay or concrete tile | Very high | 50 – 100 years | Good with a tested, fastened assembly | Premium homes with adequate framing |
| Synthetic slate / composite | Moderate–high | 30 – 50 years | Product-specific; verify wind-uplift ratings | Premium look with less weight |

**What matters most near the coast:**

- **Wind uplift** — the complete assembly, not just the covering: attachment, sheathing, ridge caps, edge metal and fasteners.
- **Corrosion** — aluminum or coated steel, stainless or hot-dipped fasteners, and flashing suited to salt exposure.
- **Water management** — underlayment, ice-and-water shield where required, sealed roof decking, correct flashing and drainage.
- **Ventilation and algae** — balanced attic ventilation and algae-resistant shingles in humid conditions.
- **Insurance and code** — confirm permit requirements and ask your carrier about FORTIFIED or wind-mitigation credits.
