**The short answer:** ask a roofing contractor for the cause of the problem and photo evidence, a written scope and itemized estimate, NC licensing and current liability and workers compensation certificates, materials and installation details, the workmanship warranty in writing, who performs the work, the schedule and cleanup plan, payment terms, and how storm damage will be documented for insurance.

The goal is not to interrogate a good contractor. It is to filter out the ones who cannot answer before money changes hands. A professional will have these answers ready.

## 1. Questions about the problem

- **What is causing the leak or damage?** “Your shingles are old” is not a diagnosis. Ask where the water enters and how they confirmed it.
- **Can you show me photos?** Before, during, and after photos protect both sides.
- **Is this isolated, or a symptom of a bigger issue?** Hidden decking, attic moisture, or widespread flashing failure changes the scope.
- **What happens if we do nothing for six months?** A truthful answer explains the risk without scare tactics.
- **Will you inspect the attic?** If accessible, the attic often shows the path and extent of water intrusion.

## 2. Questions about licensing and insurance

- Are you licensed or registered in North Carolina where required?
- Do you carry general liability insurance? What are the limits?
- Do you carry workers compensation insurance?
- Can you provide current certificates, and can I confirm them with your insurer?
- Will your own employees do the work, or will you use subcontractors?
- Who is responsible if a worker is injured on my roof?

Ask for the certificate, not a verbal yes. Then call the insurer. It takes five minutes and is the best protection you have.

## 3. Questions about the estimate and scope

- What exactly is included in the price?
- Does it include labor, materials, flashing, underlayment, decking, disposal and cleanup?
- What is excluded?
- What are your unit rates if you find rotten decking or wet insulation?
- Will you contact me for approval before doing extra work?
- Is the price fixed, or can it change?
- What deposit do you require, and when is the balance due?
- Will I get a written contract and a lien waiver?

Get at least three written estimates for the same scope when practical. A one-page estimate with a single number is not a scope.

## 4. Questions about materials and workmanship

- What materials and brands will you use?
- Will replacement shingles match the existing roof?
- What underlayment and flashing details are included?
- How will you make the repaired area watertight?
- Will the repair affect my existing roof warranty?
- How will you protect gutters, siding, landscaping and vehicles?
- What does the workmanship warranty cover, and for how long?

On the coast, also ask how they handle wind-driven rain, corrosion-resistant fasteners, and salt-air exposure.

## 5. Questions about schedule, cleanup, and payment

- When can you start, and how long will the work take?
- What happens if it rains?
- Will you use tarps or temporary protection if the roof is open?
- Who removes debris and nails, and do you magnet-sweep the yard?
- Who inspects the finished work with me?
- What is the payment schedule?
- Will you provide a lien waiver when the work is paid?

## 6. Storm damage and insurance questions

- Can you document the damage with dated photos and a written scope?
- Is the damage consistent with a storm event or normal wear?
- Will you meet my adjuster on site?
- Can you provide a detailed estimate my insurer can use?
- What temporary repairs are needed while the claim is reviewed?
- Do you charge for the inspection if I file a claim?

Be wary of anyone who promises insurance will definitely pay, asks you to sign over benefits without explaining the agreement, or pressures you to file a claim you do not need. Your insurer determines coverage.

## Red flags

- No written estimate or contract.
- Full payment requested upfront.
- Refusal to show insurance.
- A recommendation to replace the entire roof without explaining why repair is not viable.
- Pressure to decide immediately or “today only” pricing.
- No local address or references.
- A warranty that is only verbal.
- Workers who cannot tell you who they work for.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I ask a roofing contractor before hiring them for repairs?

Ask for the cause of the problem and photo evidence, a written scope and itemized estimate, NC licensing and current liability and workers compensation certificates, materials and installation details, the workmanship warranty in writing, who performs the work, the schedule and cleanup plan, payment terms, and how storm damage will be documented for insurance.

### Should I pay a roofing contractor a deposit?

A reasonable materials deposit is normal for a large repair or replacement, but full payment before work begins is not. Tie payments to milestones, keep a final payment until the work is inspected and cleaned, and ask for a written contract that states the deposit, schedule and change-order process.

### How do I know if a roofing contractor is insured?

Ask for a current certificate of insurance showing general liability and workers compensation coverage, then call the insurer to confirm the policy is active and the limits are adequate. Do not rely on a photocopy alone. If the contractor uses subcontractors, ask for their certificates too.
